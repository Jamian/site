If there is one thing software engineering teaches you early on, it’s that starting something new usually begins with a blank screen, a blinking cursor, and a disgustingly healthy dose of procrastination.

I’ve wanted to write a blog for. . . well, let’s just say a very long time. A decade ago, back when I was a fresh-faced Graduate Systems Engineer taking my first steps in tech, I put together a [quick post](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-graduate-systems-engineer-two-years-five-points-jamie-west/) looking back at my first couple of years. (note: as part of my anxiety reduction process, I have not and will not read my post from some how **ten years ago**).

I assured myself I’d keep sharing thoughts, ideas, and lessons learned along the way.

Then basically everything other than that happened, tech happened, AI happened, my kids happened and here we are ten-plus years later. But hey - better late than never.

## The 10,000-Foot Tour
If you’re wondering who’s behind the keyboard, hi! I’m Jamie.

Over the last decade and a bit, I’ve navigated the ever-shifting tides of software engineering. I started out writing code as a developer, took a detour into DevOps, found myself navigating DevSecOps, and eventually landed in the world of what I like to call Secure Platform Engineering. Basically, if it involves pipelines, cloud infrastructure, security checks, or trying to explain why the build broke at 4:55 PM on a Friday, I’ve probably been there.

Along the way, I’ve collected a fair few war stories, learned a lot of tools, and unlearned almost as many bad habits as new ones I've picked up.

##  Beyond the Terminal: Tech and Humans
While I love diving deep into infrastructure, automation, and tech stack puzzles, tech is only half the picture. The other - and frankly, more interesting half - is the humans behind the screens.

I’m also navigating life as someone who is unilaterally deaf (so if I ever appear to be ignoring you on one side, I promise it’s not personal (probably)). Beyond physical quirks, I’m open about my personal struggles with mental health. Engineering can be high-pressure, fast-paced, and modernly commonly isolating, and I’ve learned firsthand how important it is to talk about the human side of working in technology.

## What to Expect Here
This blog won't just be a series of dry "How To Configure X Tool" tutorials (no promises). Expect a mix of:

* **Tech musings**: Lessons learned from a decade in engineering, platform building, and pipeline tweaking.

* **People & culture**: Mental health, accessibility, communication, and surviving the workplace with your sanity intact.

* **Honest takes**: The fun, the chaotic, and the at this point slightly absurd realities of building modern software systems.

Whether you're a fellow engineer, a tech leader, someone navigating their own health quirks in tech, or just happened to stumble upon this corner of the internet - welcome! Grab a coffee, pull up a chair, and feel free to reach out or drop a comment.
